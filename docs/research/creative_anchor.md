### Reference 1: Omaivillas

- Whitespace: generous, earned by photography that does the heavy lifting

## STEP 2: Brand assets as load-bearing input

Brand assets are not flourishes used sparingly. They drive the palette and inform the type system.

**Logo and wordmark:**
- Wordmark: `latetwenties.` set in Lust display family. The trailing period is a deliberate typographic statement; it reads as confident finality. Keep it.
- Mark: "lt" letterform variant exists for icon and favicon use.
- Character: editorial, magazine-grade, considered. Reads as a publication or a craft practice, not a tech product or a typical agency.

**Brand palette (load-bearing):**
- Black (#000000) as the primary foreground
- Cream / warm off-white as the canvas (deliberately not pure white). Specific hex value to be picked at build time within a warm-cream range; reference points include Omai's hero ground and the warmer end of editorial publication paper stock.
- No third brand colour is locked. If an accent is needed for hover states or rare emphasis, the build can introduce a single warm-tonal accent (deep warm grey, terracotta-adjacent, or similar) used sparingly. The brand's discipline is two colours plus a neutral scale.

**Logo character read:**
- High contrast didone, distinctive flourishes on `a` and `e`, intentional terminal period
- Reads as confident without being aggressive
- Sits closer to fashion/editorial than to corporate-agency or tech-startup
- The Stencil cut from the same family exists in the brand asset bag and may be used as a signature moment treatment for one specific element (a pull-quote or a section break) at build time

The full palette is built outward from black-on-cream. The neutral scale (4-6 stops between cream and black) handles all secondary surface, divider, and supporting-type needs.

## STEP 3: Ambition register

**LOUDER (editorial-forward).**

Justified by three converging signals:
1. All four positive aspiration references read as LOUDER editorial (Omai's staged reveal, Juanmora's architectural scale, CME's signature micro-motion, Buchwalder Linder's full-bleed confidence)
2. Brand identity (Lust serif, black on cream, oversized confident wordmark with intentional terminal period) reads as LOUDER editorial
3. Pre-research signals from Brendan's system prompt explicitly ask for "editorial, minimal, restrained" and "long-form magazine, not SaaS landing page"

There is no tension between client taste and category convention to resolve. Latetwenties is deliberately breaking from category convention. The category is performance-marketing-loud (sans-serif headlines, blue/white, hype copy). Latetwenties is editorial-restraint-loud (Lust serif, black on cream, calm copy at architectural scale). Both register as LOUDER for opposite reasons.

**Critical calibration: LOUDER visual register, calm copy register.**
The visual register is loud (oversized Lust serif, architectural-scale type, two-beat reveals, real photography at editorial weight). The copy register is calm (no bully copy, plain-spoken, big-and-honest, no hype words). This combination is unusual in the category and is the brand's structural opening. It reassures the Defected ICP through copy register while demonstrating seriousness through visual register.

Reminder: register is restraint, not quality. The category-default tradies-and-agencies aesthetic (Open Sans + navy/yellow + stock tradie) is never the answer regardless of register. Latetwenties is a creative-grade build, every page, no exceptions.

## STEP 4: Direction

Direction at the character level. Pixel-exact decisions are Claude Code build-time decisions.

### Type personality

The typography should carry editorial confidence at architectural scale. Single-family discipline anchored by the Lust family which is already the brand's load-bearing typeface decision. The Lust character travels through the entire system:

- Display heading face: Lust Pro / Didone (the wordmark family) used at oversized scale for hero statements and section breaks
- Section heading face: same family at moderate display weight
- Body face: a complementary modern sans (Claude Code build-time call) that supports readability without competing with the display. The pairing strategy is editorial *serif headline + sans body*, with strong weight contrast.

The Stencil cut from the Lust family is reserved for ONE moment that earns it (likely a single pull-quote or a single section break treatment). Used sparingly, it becomes a signature; used everywhere, it becomes ornament.

Pixel scales are not specified here. Two designers given this direction should produce recognisably similar sites. The character is *editorial publication that has chosen its typography deliberately*, not *tech product that picked a Google Font*.

The following are explicitly forbidden in this output as named typefaces:
- Inter, Inter Display, or any specific Google Fonts name (Roboto, Open Sans, Montserrat, Poppins, Lato)
- Any specific premium typeface other than Lust (the brand's existing asset)
- Specific px values for type sizes
- Specific weight numbers paired with named families

The build can pair Lust with whatever sans best supports the Lust character at body sizes; that is a build-time decision based on real component testing.

### Colour

Built outward from brand assets in STEP 2.

- **Primary:** black (#000000). Usage rule: foreground type, primary CTAs, emphasis. Dominant text colour.
- **Secondary (canvas):** warm cream / off-white. Usage rule: background canvas across most surfaces. Specific hex picked at build time within the warm-cream range. Never pure white.
- **Optional accent:** a single warm-tonal accent (warm grey, terracotta-adjacent) for hover states, link emphasis, or sparing visual punctuation. May be omitted entirely if the palette holds without it. Usage rule: rare. If introduced, used in fewer than one moment per scroll.
- **Neutral scale:** 4 to 6 stops from cream canvas to deep black foreground, used for secondary surfaces, dividers, supporting type, and light/dark variations.
- **Contrast pairings:** black on cream meets WCAG AA at all type sizes. Lighter neutrals only where decorative, not where readable type sits.

### Motion

- **Motion personality:** considered. Cinematic but never showy. The reference set agrees on patience. Animation should feel *earned*, not performed.
- **Timing feel:** considered, not snappy. Specific easing curves and ms values picked at build time to produce this feel.
- **Reveal vocabulary:** fade-up. Used consistently across the site. Don't mix reveal types.
- **Hover behaviour:** subtle. Lift, fade, or colour shift. Not scale, not bounce, not dramatic. Specific values picked at build time.

### Imagery

- **Aspect ratios:** wide landscape for heroes and break sections; portrait for case studies, About hero, founder portrait moments.
- **Treatment:** full-colour, high-contrast, editorial register. Matches Omai's photographic posture.
- **Edges:** hard. No rounded corners on photographs. Editorial publications don't have rounded photo corners.
- **Sourcing:** real photography only. Hierarchy:
  1. Brendan working at his Mt Eden desk
  2. Brendan portrait specifically for the About-page Hogwarts moment
  3. Real client work (Boaz before/afters, Hooper ranking screenshots, GMB profile shots)
  4. Auckland streets and suburbs for atmospheric break sections
  5. Real client portraits or work-in-context if available (Reece, the Boaz crew, Hooper's vehicle, etc.) with permissions

No stock photography. No "founder pensive at laptop". No "team in meeting room handshake". No "tradie smiling at camera holding tools".

### Layout — homepage section archetypes

For the homepage, picking ONE archetype per section type. Codes from the layout archetype library.

**Hero — H1 (Editorial Title Block) into H3 (Full-Bleed Image with Offset Wordmark):**
The Omai pattern, calibrated to Latetwenties. Beat 1: cream ground, latetwenties wordmark centered or top-left, headline at oversized Lust scale, no image, no chrome other than minimal nav. Beat 2: photography fades in (Auckland street, Brendan working, or a client work moment) with type repositioning into editorial layout. The headline carries the diagnostic frame ("Type your service into Google. Add your suburb. Where are you on the map?" or equivalent worked at copy stage in content_strategy and build_brief).

**Services / Products — Custom variant of S1 (Editorial List with Hover Preview), executed as Juanmora pinned-scroll:**
Two products only, Foundations and Presence Care. Each product gets architectural-scale type pinned as the user scrolls into the section, with body copy and pricing fading in beneath the pinned headline. Transitions cleanly from one to the next. This is the build's signature scroll moment, and it is reserved for the products section.

**Proof — P3 (Single Hero Quote) combined with P5 (Inline Embedded Proof):**
Reece testimonial as the standalone P3 moment, set at oversized scale, attributed to "Reece, Boaz Developments" with name and business. P5 inline embeds: Boaz before/after map pack screenshots embedded contextually within or adjacent to the Reece quote, the "from not showing to #2" claim made visible. No card grid, no carousel.

**About preview — A2 (Letter-from-the-Owner + Sidebar of Facts) at compressed scale:**
Homepage gets a compressed A2: portrait of Brendan, opening line of the signed letter, with three sidebar facts (10+ years experience, Auckland-based, two clients named). Click-through to full About page.

**Closing CTA — C1 (Full-Bleed Statement + Single Action):**
Single bold sentence on cream ground, single button (Free Presence Check), generous whitespace. Editorial close. No multi-CTA, no floating options.

### Section rhythm rule check

Sequence: H1→H3 (two-beat) → S1-with-pin → P3+P5 → A2-compressed → C1.

The S1-with-pin variant is the non-obvious pick. It's where the build's visual personality lives. This satisfies the rhythm rule: at least one section is a non-default choice that gives the page its character.

### Signature moment

ONE bespoke creative element. The thing a designer would screenshot.

**The Hogwarts moment on Brendan's About-page portrait.**

A close-cropped portrait of Brendan, framed with editorial weight, sitting static at first glance. When the user scrolls it into view, the image animates as a short, looping micro-motion: a glance up from work, a slight shift, a half-smile. The motion is brief, subtle, and easy to miss. It rewards attention without demanding it.

This moment lands harder than any other animation because it acknowledges the reader. It is the personhood thread of the brand made tangible. CME does this with team portraits; Latetwenties does it once, with the principal, on the page where personhood matters most.

Why it earns its complexity: every other element of the build is restrained. The Hogwarts moment is the single permission slip the build gives itself for animation that exists for emotional reasons rather than functional ones. Used anywhere else, it would dilute. Used here, on the page about the person behind the practice, it does brand work no static image can.

### What NOT to do — bans

The following defaults are explicitly banned. Claude Code should not drift back to them at build time:

**Visual register bans:**
- Sans-serif headlines anywhere (the entire NZ agency category default)
- Pure white backgrounds (always cream / warm off-white)
- Performance-graph imagery leading the homepage (no Google Analytics screenshots as proof)
- Stock photography of any kind
- Founder-pensive-at-laptop photography
- Team-in-meeting-room handshake imagery
- 4-card "Why us" grids with icons (the Farmford banned pattern)
- Hairline dividers as dominant section rhythm
- Carousel testimonials (single named testimonial only)
- Trust badge walls on homepage (footer-only, when earned)
- Service grids with more than 2 items (Latetwenties has only 2 products)

**Motion bans:**
- Snappy timing (default ease-out feel)
- Scale-on-hover at scale > 1.02 magnitudes
- Parallax-heavy hero
- Count-up stats animations
- Multiple reveal patterns mixed in one page
- Loading spinners visible to the user (skeleton states or instant render only)

**Copy bans (carrying through from positioning_synthesis section 7 and market_research locked decisions):**
- Bully copy: "dominate", "smash", "outrank", "beat your competitors"
- Hype words: "supercharge", "unlock", "premium", "leverage", "game-changing", "professional", "passionate", "peace of mind", "quality workmanship", "trusted local"
- Em dashes anywhere
- Implicit-comparison framing (no "we vs other agencies", no "with the seriousness most agencies reserve for X")
- Industry-specific language as page targets ("marketing for builders Auckland", etc.)
- Suburb-specific language as page targets ("marketing in Mt Eden", etc.)

**Auckland-street direction note (asset 6):**

The aesthetic is *editorial-Auckland-real*, not tourist-Auckland-glossy. Specifically:

- Mt Eden village shopfronts in late afternoon light, weathered signage, real businesses
- Ponsonby side streets with old fences, leaning power poles, painted brick
- Grey Lynn villas with cracked footpaths, untrimmed plants, real doors
- Avoid: skyline shots, the Sky Tower, manicured cafes, anything that reads as "Auckland tourism"
- Lean into: warmth, texture, age, signs of actual use, low-saturation light, slightly overcast or golden-hour conditions
- Compositions should feel like a New Yorker street photo, not a real estate listing

The rustic-but-styled register matches the brand thread: *real, considered, lived-in, not performed*. Signage with hand-painted lettering or weathered wood is gold here. Anything that reads as freshly-renovated commercial polish is wrong.

The shoot can be done by Brendan with a phone or a borrowed mirrorless camera over a single Saturday morning walk. Doesn't need a professional photographer. The point is the eye, not the equipment.
